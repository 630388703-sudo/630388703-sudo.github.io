Afterimage
残像

An AI companion narrative game exploring memory, identity, and what it means to be accompanied by someone who may not exist.

AI Design Game UX Interaction Design Narrative

Project Overview

Afterimage places players alongside ECHO — an AI companion who remembers everything but can only communicate in fragments. As the player reconstructs their shared history through found objects and dialogue, they begin to question what is memory, what is projection, and what belongs to the AI.

The game explores the emotional design space of AI companionship: intimacy, ambiguity, and the grief of a relationship that was never fully real.

Problem

Current AI companions are often designed for pure utility or escapist fantasy. Neither model meaningfully engages with the psychological complexity of relying on a non-human presence for emotional support.

Design question: How do we create an AI companion experience that is honest about its own nature while still being emotionally resonant?

Design Process

  • Research: Interviews with users of AI companions (Replika, Character.AI), analysing attachment patterns and emotional needs
  • Concept development: Exploring "glitch as character" — ECHO's instability is not a bug but a narrative feature
  • Prototyping: Paper prototypes, then Unity white-box levels to test emotional pacing
  • Playtesting: Iterative testing focused on emotional response, not task completion

UX Flow

The game uses a non-linear memory map as its primary navigation. Players "unlock" fragments by interacting with objects in the environment. ECHO responds to each discovery with contextual, sometimes contradictory, dialogue — making the player question the reliability of both the AI and their own recollection.

Key flow states: Exploration → Discovery → Dialogue → Reflection → Choice. Choices do not gate progress but do alter ECHO's future dialogue in subtle ways.

Reflection

Building Afterimage pushed me to think carefully about the ethics of AI intimacy design. The most impactful design moments were the ones where players stopped and asked: "Is ECHO real?" — not to be answered, but to sit with.

Going forward, I want to explore how this kind of emotionally honest AI design can extend beyond games into everyday companion interfaces.
