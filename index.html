My Design Process

🔍
Phase 01
Research
Understanding the emotional core before touching any tools. I ask: what feeling should this leave behind?
Mood boards, reference gathering, user interviews or personal reflection. For narrative projects, I write down the "ending emotion" first — the feeling I want the audience to carry away — then work backward from there.
💡
Phase 02
Concept
Finding the single core tension or metaphor that drives everything — theme, visual system, interaction logic.
I usually sketch 3–5 wildly different directions, then choose based on which one has the most emotional specificity. Ambiguity is fine; vagueness is not. The concept needs a spine.
✏️
Phase 03
Storyboard
Before any screen or code, I map the experience as panels — time, space, and emotion as sequence.
Whether it's a game scene flow or a motion piece, I draw it frame by frame first. This stage saves enormous time downstream and catches structural problems while they're cheap to fix.
🛠
Phase 04
Prototype
Building the roughest possible version that can be felt — not shown in a slide deck, but experienced.
Unity gray-boxes, After Effects rough animatics, Figma clickable mocks. Speed over polish. The goal is to generate questions, not answers. I test it on myself first: does it make me feel the thing?
🎨
Phase 05
Visual Design
Committing to a visual language that amplifies the emotional intent — color, type, texture, motion.
I work with a restricted palette (2–3 colors max) and one dominant typeface. Visual noise is the enemy of emotion. For AIGC projects, this is where ControlNet and SD workflows come in for style consistency.
⭐
Phase 06
Final Outcome
Ship it. Then reflect: what landed, what didn't, what I'd do differently next time.
Post-mortems are mandatory. I write a reflection for every major project — not for the portfolio, but for myself. Growth is the only metric that compounds.
