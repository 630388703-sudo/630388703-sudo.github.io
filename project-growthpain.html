Project Overview

Growth Pain / 生长痛 is a visual novel about the invisible emotional weight carried by students navigating academic pressure, parental expectations, and the anxiety of growing up in a competitive system. The protagonist, Jiang Xun, is a high school student facing the same pressures shared by millions — but rarely spoken about.

The game is my Portfolio Piece No.1 — the work I'm most proud of as a narrative designer. It exists at the intersection of interaction design, emotional storytelling, and social commentary.

"You can't choose where you start. But the path you take — that's yours."

Design Problem

Academic pressure is one of the most pervasive emotional experiences for young people in East Asia — yet it's rarely depicted with nuance in interactive media. Most portrayals either dramatise it for effect or trivialise it. I wanted to ask:

❓
Design Question
How might a visual novel let players feel the weight of invisible emotional pressure — without telling them how to feel about it?
Narrative Challenge
Show, Don't Tell

The emotional state of the protagonist had to be communicated through environmental storytelling, dialogue subtext, and UI choices — not explicit internal monologue. The player should arrive at empathy through inference, not instruction.

System Challenge
Choice Without Judgment

Most branching visual novels signal 'correct' choices through tone or reward. Growth Pain needed to resist this — every path had to feel valid, even contradictory ones. The value system had to be invisible until the very end.

03

Narrative Structure

The game unfolds across six acts, each set in a distinct location that carries its own emotional atmosphere. The scenes were chosen not just as backdrops but as active participants in the storytelling — the classroom's fluorescent silence, the milk tea shop's brief freedom, the gym's forced camaraderie.

ACT I
Classroom
Morning · Before Class

The day begins. Jiang Xun sits in silence as pressure accumulates before a single word is spoken.

ACT II
Teacher's Office
After Exam Results

A confrontation with authority. The gap between what adults say and what they mean becomes visible.

ACT III
Milk Tea Shop
After School

A brief reprieve. Friendship, small choices, the feeling of being — briefly — just a person.

ACT IV
Living Room
Evening · Home

The family space. Words left unsaid. The distance between people who love each other.

ACT V
Study Room
Late Night

Alone with the work. The weight of time. The question of whether any of it means anything.

ACT VI
Gymnasium
Graduation Day

The ending — but not a conclusion. Six different ways to leave the same building.

04

Hidden Value System

The branching system is driven by four hidden value axes — invisible to the player, accumulating silently with every choice. The player never sees a score or a meter. They only feel the consequences in Act VI, when Jiang Xun's path resolves into one of six endings.

Identity (认同感)
How much does Jiang Xun value external approval?

Tracks choices that seek validation from teachers, parents, and peers versus choices that prioritise internal motivation.

Time (时间感)
How does Jiang Xun relate to the irreversibility of choices?

Tracks whether the player dwells on past decisions, rushes forward, or sits with uncertainty — directly tied to the 'temporal irreversibility' theme.

Pressure (压力值)
How much has accumulated — and what does Jiang Xun do with it?

Unlike the others, this axis can only increase. Choices may slow or redirect the accumulation, but never eliminate it — mirroring the systemic nature of academic pressure.

Self (自我感)
Does Jiang Xun know who she is, outside of the system?

The quietest axis. Rises when Jiang Xun chooses moments of genuine self-expression. The ending most players miss is the one where this axis is highest.

💡
Key Design Decision
The value axes are never shown to the player. This was a deliberate choice — the moment you show someone a score, they optimise for it rather than playing authentically. The system only works if it's invisible.
05

Visual Design

Character sprites were generated using Stable Diffusion with IP-Adapter + Lineart ControlNet — ensuring visual consistency across all six scenes while allowing stylistic variation. The colour palette shifts gradually across acts, moving from cooler, more desaturated tones in early acts toward warmer but more fragmented palettes toward the end.

Scene renders — Act III (Milk Tea Shop) and environment study

Character Design
SD + IP-Adapter

Stable Diffusion IP-Adapter maintained consistent character appearance across all sprite variations — expressions, costumes, postures — without manual illustration for each.

Line Control
Lineart ControlNet

Lineart ControlNet ensured clean outlines and consistent anatomy across generated images. Combined with img2img refinement for expression-specific adjustments.

Motion PV
After Effects

A promotional PV was produced in After Effects — typewriter text animations, sprite highlight transitions, scene composition with dynamic music sync across 11 sub-compositions.

Promotional PV

A 3.5-minute motion PV was produced alongside the game as a standalone portfolio piece. Built in After Effects across 11 sub-compositions at 1920×1080/30fps, it uses typewriter expressions, Venetian Blinds transitions, and Math.sin-driven animations to convey the emotional arc of the full game in compressed form.

Design Process

01
Research — Social Issue Grounding

The design began with personal reflection and secondary research into academic pressure among East Asian youth — existing studies, personal interviews, and observed behaviour patterns. This shaped which emotional truths the game needed to honour.

User ResearchSocial Issue
02
Script — Six-Act Branching Structure

The full six-act script was written in English (for portfolio clarity) with complete dialogue for each scene, branch points annotated with their value axis effects, and all six ending conditions documented. This was the most time-intensive phase — roughly 3 months.

Script WritingBranching Logic
03
Character Design — AI-Assisted Pipeline

Character sprites were generated using a Stable Diffusion pipeline: initial design concept → IP-Adapter for style consistency → Lineart ControlNet for structural control → img2img refinement → manual background removal via OpenCV flood-fill in Python.

Stable DiffusionControlNetPython / OpenCV
04
UI Layout — Scene Composition in Figma

Each scene's visual layout was prototyped in Figma — dialogue box positioning, character placement, background layering, and choice UI presentation. The goal was to ensure readability without sacrificing atmosphere.

FigmaUI Layout
05
Motion PV — After Effects Production

The promotional PV was produced last — as a synthesis of all elements into a single coherent emotional arc. 11 sub-compositions, typewriter expressions (via AE expressions), Venetian Blinds transitions, and wiggle/Math.sin animations timed to the BGM.

08

Outcome & Reflection

Growth Pain is the project where I found my voice as a narrative designer. It taught me that the most powerful design decisions are often the ones you remove — the scores you hide, the instructions you don't give, the endings you make the player discover for themselves.

It's also the project that clarified what I want to do with interactive design: create experiences that make people feel understood — not entertained, not impressed, but genuinely seen.

  • Designed a complete six-act branching script with four hidden value axes
  • Built a full AIGC character design pipeline (SD + ControlNet + Python)
  • Produced a 3.5-min motion PV entirely in After Effects
  • Designed six scene layouts with UI components prototyped in Figma
  • First portfolio piece for graduate school applications abroad
🔮
What I'd Do Differently
I'd prototype the ending conditions earlier — specifically, I'd playtest the value system with at least five people before locking in the script. The axis weights were largely intuitive, and some balance issues only became visible after the full script was written. Earlier playtesting would have caught these sooner.
